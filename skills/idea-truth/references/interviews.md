# User conversations that produce facts, not compliments

Principles below follow the widely used "Mom Test" approach (Rob Fitzpatrick's book of that name): talk about the
person's life and past behaviour, not your idea; ask for specifics, not opinions; listen more than talk.

## Rules

- Do not pitch until the end (or at all). The moment you describe the idea, answers turn polite.
- Ask about the LAST TIME it happened, not "usually" or "would you".
- Dig into numbers: how long, how much, how often, who else was involved.
- Every compliment or "great idea" is noise. Every complaint about the status quo is signal; ask what they did about it.
- 5 to 10 conversations per user type. Stop when answers repeat.
- Write notes the same day: quotes, actions, numbers. No summaries like "they loved it".

## Script (15 minutes)

1. "Tell me about the last time you <job> ." (situation, date, place)
2. "What was hardest about it?" / "Why was that hard?"
3. "What did you do about it?" (workaround = strongest signal)
4. "How much time or money did that take?"
5. "What have you tried to fix it? Why did you stop using it?"
6. "Who else deals with this? Can you introduce me?" (a yes = real interest)
7. Only now, optional: show the demo, then ask "would you use this next time <job> happens? Can I check with you then?"

## What counts as evidence

| Signal | Strength |
|---|---|
| Paid money for a workaround, or would pre-pay now | strong |
| Spends time on a workaround every week | strong |
| Gives an introduction, a document, a real example | medium |
| Agrees to a follow-up on a date | medium |
| "Sounds useful", "I would use it", survey tick | none |

## Online substitute (no time for calls, e.g. hackathon)

Read 10 to 20 public posts in the users' own words (Reddit, X, Play Store reviews of rival apps, government grievance
portals, YouTube comments). Record: link, date, the quote, what they did about it. Count distinct people, not posts.
