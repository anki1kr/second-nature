# launch-legal

Helps founders and engineers build and audit product legal basics: a privacy policy, terms of use, a cookie/consent architecture, and data protection compliance checklists for India (DPDP Act 2023), the European Union (GDPR), the United Kingdom (UK GDPR + PECR), and the United States, California (CCPA/CPRA).

## Universal Legal Notice Rule

Every document, checklist, or text generated by this skill MUST display this disclaimer directly beneath the document title:

> *Disclaimer: This document is an operational reference draft and does not constitute legal advice. Have a qualified attorney admitted to practice in your jurisdiction review and approve all terms, policies, and notices before publishing or accepting user data.*

---

## Non-Negotiable Operational Rules

1. **Official Legal Sources Only:** Every statutory requirement must cite its section/article and link to the confirmed official source:
   - **India:** DPDP Act 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023), published in Gazette of India Extraordinary No. 25, August 11, 2023. Official MeitY text: `https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf`.
   - **DPDP Rules Status:** PIB reported notification on November 14, 2025 with an 18-month phased timeline, but the live official Gazette rule text could not be verified directly during testing. All subordinate rule details must be written as *"check with a lawyer"*, never as an absolute rule.
   - **European Union:** GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) on `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679`; ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC on `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32002L0058`.
   - **United Kingdom:** UK GDPR, Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018), and PECR 2003 via Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) on `https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/` and `https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/`. Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA) amendments on `https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/`.
   - **United States (California):** CCPA as amended by CPRA (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.) and CPPA Regulations (Title 11, Division 6 CCR § 7000 et seq.) on `https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa`, `https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa/gpc`, `https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/consumer_privacy_act.html`, and `https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/20230329_final_regs_text.pdf`.
   - Any unverified legal point must state *"check with a lawyer"*.
2. **Zero Fact Hallucination:** Never assume or invent facts about the product. Read the codebase (config files, dependencies, environment variables, authentication setup, database schemas, analytics tags, payment webhooks) or ask the user directly. Unknown details must remain open questions.
3. **Flag High-Risk Clauses:** Any clause dependent on corporate domicile, specialized regulation (health, banking, education), or high liability exposure must be tagged `[⚠️ LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Intake & Codebase Audit
Before drafting a single line, identify the factual operational footprint:
1. **Operating Entity:** Legal entity name, registered office, jurisdiction of incorporation, primary contact email.
2. **User Demographics:** Individuals or businesses? Does the app serve users in India, the EU/EEA, the UK, the US (California), or globally? Are minors (under 18 in India per DPDP Sec. 2(f); under 16 in EU per GDPR Art. 8; under 13 in UK per DPA 2018 Sec. 9; under 16 in CA per Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.120(c)) permitted or targeted?
3. **Data Categories:**
   - *Direct:* Name, email, passwords, phone numbers, uploaded profiles.
   - *Automatic:* IP addresses, device headers, user-agent strings, session telemetry.
   - *Cookies / Storage:* Essential auth tokens, analytics cookies, marketing tags.
   - *Sensitive / Special Categories:* Financial data, government IDs, biometric data, minor data.
4. **Third-Party Processors:** Cloud hosting (AWS, GCP, Supabase), auth providers (Clerk, Auth0, Firebase), payment gateways (Stripe, Razorpay), analytics (PostHog, Google Analytics), transactional email (Resend, SendGrid).
5. **Commercial Terms:** Free vs paid tiers, recurring billing/auto-renewal mechanisms, refund windows, user-generated content (UGC) capabilities.

If any factual item is missing from the codebase or user prompt, halt drafting and output an **Intake Question Table**.

---

### Step 2: Privacy Policy Generation
Draft a plain-language privacy notice structured in the following order:
1. **Entity Identification & Contact:** Legal entity name, address, and DPO or privacy contact email (DPDP Sec. 8(9); GDPR Art. 13(1)(a)-(b)).
2. **Itemised Data Mapping:** Concrete table listing each data element, its specific collection purpose, and its documented legal basis (GDPR Art. 6(1); DPDP Sec. 5(1) & Sec. 6(1)):
   - Avoid vague statements like *"to improve our services"*. State the exact operational purpose.
3. **Third-Party Subprocessors:** Clear disclosure of third-party processors and their specific role (DPDP Sec. 8(2); GDPR Art. 13(1)(e)).
4. **Data Retention Schedule:** Concrete retention timelines per category (e.g., active account + 90 days; billing records for 7 years per statutory tax requirements) (DPDP Sec. 8(7); GDPR Art. 13(2)(a)).
5. **User Rights & Execution:** Plain-language guide on how users exercise rights: access, rectification, erasure, and nomination (DPDP Sec. 11, 12, 14; GDPR Art. 15-22). Include the right to lodge complaints with the Data Protection Board of India or competent EU Supervisory Authority.
6. **Children's Privacy Protection:** Clear notice on minor consent. If minors are served, specify verifiable parental consent (DPDP Sec. 9(1); GDPR Art. 8) and confirm absolute prohibition on tracking/behavioral ads directed at children (DPDP Sec. 9(3)).

---

### Step 3: Terms of Use Generation (Feature-Module Architecture)
Assemble the terms agreement module-by-module based on active product capabilities:
1. **Acceptance & Modification:** Require clear notification and a specified notice period (e.g., 30 days) before material changes take effect. Avoid bare *"continued use constitutes agreement"* clauses.
2. **Account Registration:** Age minimums, credential security, account sharing bans, and suspension criteria.
3. **Payment & Subscriptions Module (if applicable):**
   - Explicit price presentation, tax handling, and billing cycles.
   - **Auto-Renewal Rule:** Must specify pre-charge reminder timing and an immediate, self-serve cancellation method accessible directly from user account settings.
   - Refund rules, chargeback consequences, and statutory cooling-off periods.
4. **User-Generated Content (UGC) Module (if applicable):**
   - Non-exclusive IP license granted to the platform solely for hosting and displaying content.
   - Clear community guidelines, prohibited content list, moderation turnaround, and user appeal procedures.
5. **Limitation of Liability & Warranty Disclaimers:**
   - Both disclaimer of consequential/indirect damages AND a concrete monetary liability cap (e.g., `[amounts paid in preceding 12 months or INR 5,000 / €100 / £100 / $100, whichever is greater]`). Never omit the monetary cap.
6. **Termination & Dispute Resolution:** Termination rights for both parties, governing law, and competent courts/arbitration forum.

---

### Step 4: Cookie & Consent Architecture
Specify frontend consent logic matching regulatory requirements:
1. **ePrivacy & PECR Opt-In:** Under ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3) and UK PECR Reg. 6, non-essential cookies (analytics, heatmaps, tracking) MUST NOT execute or set cookies before the user clicks an affirmative "Accept" button. (For California, provide a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link and honor Global Privacy Control under 11 CCR § 7025).
2. **No Bundling / Pre-Ticked Boxes:** Under DPDP Sec. 6(1), GDPR Art. 7(4), and UK GDPR Art. 7(4), consent toggles must default to unchecked. Terms acceptance cannot be bundled with marketing or analytics tracking.
3. **Ease of Withdrawal:** Users must have a permanent settings link (e.g., in the footer) to change cookie preferences or withdraw consent with equal ease (DPDP Sec. 6(4); GDPR Art. 7(3); UK GDPR Art. 7(3); CCPA 11 CCR § 7026).
